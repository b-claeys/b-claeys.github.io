Personal Project

I built a compression-sleeve prototype that records muscle activity and arm motion together during exercise. Three EMG channels, two IMUs, and a direct elbow-angle sensor feed a Bluetooth dashboard for comparing the signals during curls, extensions, and lateral raises. My work covered the sensing layout, wearable hardware, electrode mounting, calibration, and integrated testing.

Project Overview

Exercise Tests I used biceps curls, triceps extensions, and lateral raises to compare muscle activity with elbow motion, arm orientation, and repetition timing.
EMG Three BioAmp EXG Pill channels measuring biceps, triceps, and shoulder activity through separate electrode pairs with a shared reference near the elbow.
Motion + Joint Angle A shoulder-mounted LSM6DS3TR-C and the elbow module's onboard IMU provide segment orientation, with a potentiometer on A3 measuring elbow flexion directly. Entered upper-arm and forearm lengths are used to estimate elbow and wrist position.
Electrode Contact + Placement I used removable EMG pods to control electrode spacing and placement, then adjusted pod fit, contact pressure, and wire routing to keep the electrodes stable as the arm moved.
Wearable Hardware Compression sleeve carrying the EMG pods, shoulder sensor, routed wiring, and an elbow module containing the controller, power hardware, and elbow-angle sensor.
Processing + Output XIAO nRF52840 Sense sampling and processing the sensor inputs, tracking repetition phase, estimating arm position, and sending the results over Bluetooth Low Energy to the live dashboard.

Wearable Hardware

The main electronics pod houses the controller and power hardware near the elbow, with flexible wiring running to the sensing pods positioned along the sleeve. The complete pod assembly attaches with sew-on snap fasteners, so it can be removed as one unit and repositioned between attachment points to suit different arm sizes and sensor locations.

Elbow-Angle Sensor Cap

Early concepts used gold-plated PCB-style contact pieces, but the pod design moved toward flexible TPU and rounded contact surfaces so the interface could follow the sleeve and distribute pressure more evenly. The final geometry keeps each electrode pair about 2 cm apart, with removable caps holding the contacts in place. A fixed reference electrode near the elbow gives a consistent location with less movement, while the wire routing keeps cable motion away from the electrode sites.

Testing

Testing started with each sensor independently before moving into full exercise trials. This exposed channel-mapping and intermittent IMU issues and showed how strongly EMG quality changed with electrode pressure and cable movement.

I corrected the channel mapping and IMU configuration so EMG, orientation, and elbow angle could stream together during movement. The current version still shows a slight delay between motion and the dashboard response, along with noticeable noise in the EMG signals.
